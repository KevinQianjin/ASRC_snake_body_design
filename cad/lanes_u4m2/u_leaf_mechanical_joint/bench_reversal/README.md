# U1 leaf joint reversal bench check

The first coupon layout puts a seated M1.6 screw head in the proximal compression load path. The countersunk head contacts the shoe at its nominal starting position. The shoe has another 0.12 mm to travel before the PA12 lip engages. The 0.49 mm proximal travel from the first fit study applies when the screw head and neighbouring face both clear that shoe motion. With the screw fixed at its modeled seated position, nominal proximal free travel is **0.37 mm**, with initial compression routed into the fastener. Use the original coupons for low-force diagnosis and revise the proximal retention geometry before a service-load compression trial.

## Contact order and low-force response

The model uses the positive-X, positive-Y NiTi blade and the two U1 coupon solids derived from published module CAD. Local depth increases into each module end. The geometry check translates one part along the specified axis to bracket contact with a 0.0002 mm search interval and a 0.00001 mm³ intersection threshold. Across 63 aligned positions through the allowed travel, the solver found zero overlap between the coupon, blade and retainers within the stated threshold. The seated screw head overlaps the shoe by 0.446 mm³ when the shoe is forced to the nominal lip position in the rigid model.

The fit datum is 96.00 mm between faces. The two end travels add when the fixture closes or opens. The plateaus in the trace represent unpreloaded, frictionless geometry with rigid stops. The elastic branches use the actual bowed NiTi free span with end rotations fixed and the repository's 40 GPa elastic assumption. Joint compliance, screw torque, fixture compliance, print tolerances, friction and material hysteresis each require hardware measurements. A nominal seated screw path with effective axial stiffness above 41.7 N/mm would still leave the lip open under 5 N in a linear first-contact model. This threshold follows from 5 N divided by 0.12 mm. The actual fastener-path stiffness is unknown.

At −5 N the free span shortens by 0.0334 mm in the model, yielding face separation of 95.8466 mm with the seated screw and 95.7266 mm with the pin. At +5 N the span extends 0.0289 mm and both fixtures reach 96.5289 mm. The 1.00 mm and 0.50 mm section spacing runs agreed within 0.000001 mm for the free-span changes at the eight nonzero load levels. Use force limits and measured motion on the stand.

The original base and an unmodified preceding U distal face both meet the shoe after 0.08 mm of outward shoe travel with the head constraint removed. At the lip stop the shoe projects 0.04 mm past the mating plane and overlaps either rigid mating CAD by approximately 1.916 mm³. The open-face pin test clears this region on the test stand. A stacked joint needs a recessed load-bearing shoe that clears the retainer head through the measured production tolerance range. [Original base CAD](../../guides_v2/base_interface.stl)

## Run the physical comparison

1. **Inspect and log both coupons.** Use the PA12 process and orientation intended for the full module, a real 94 mm NiTi blade, metal keys and a metal backing shoe. Check the 0.70 mm key-slot wall, 0.82 mm nut-pocket roof, 1.48 mm lip and 1.00 mm distal floor at the sections called out in the first fit study. Inspect the slots for trapped powder, damage or an edge that catches the blade. Record actual dimensions, lot, conditioning and instrument identification. [Specimen worksheet](specimen_checks.csv)
2. **Mount and align.** Fix the proximal coupon by its test flange. Put the distal coupon on an anti-rotation linear slide with travel in both directions from the 96 mm setup datum. Align the two blade end seats using the original XY coordinates. Centre the applied force through the selected blade centreline at the seats. That line is offset from the fixture-hole row. Use stiff fixture plates and leave the outer face and side of the proximal shoe open. With a dummy rigid link record fixture lost motion and verify the force and displacement zeros. Aim for 0.005 mm or better displacement resolution and 0.05 N or better force resolution. A calibrated camera or displacement sensors should separately track the blade at each seat, the proximal shoe and the screw head or pin relative to local PA12 references.
3. **Run A with the seated screw.** Verify hardware dimensions against the drawing and record the actual seat depth and assembly torque. Drive slowly through tension and compression in displacement control, limiting the first three reversals to ±2 N. Inspect the parts and repeat for three cycles at ±5 N if seating is repeatable. Mark when the shoe first moves and when force rises. Stop on a migrating screw or pin, bowing that changes the load axis, cracking, permanent movement or a force excursion. [Trace template](trace_template.csv)
4. **Run B with the diagnostic pin.** Fully unload. Remove the proximal screw and square nut and secure a smooth, measured Ø1.60 mm pin coaxially in an external holder. Its tip should extend 5.80 mm into the coupon from the datum face. The pin centre is x = 6.40 mm and y = 21.12 mm. Leave the holder and shoulder clear of the shoe and coupon face. The straight CAD verifies zero pin insertion overlap and captures both the shoe and key against translation in their withdrawal direction. Hold the pin axially during the test and track the pin marker. Follow the same ±2 N and ±5 N sequence. Refit the screw and repeat A to check whether reassembly or permanent set changed the result.
5. **Reduce the measurements.** From each cycle use the settled loading ramp on each side of zero, once the contact markers confirm seating. Fit local displacement versus force on the compressive and tensile ramps separately. Extrapolate both fits to zero force and subtract the intercepts to estimate backlash. Use the inverse slope magnitude for branch stiffness and report the fit range, cycle scatter, fixture check and instrument resolution. Record the shoe and head motion at each change in slope. If the shoe reaches the lip in B and stays off the lip in A at the same force, the comparison isolates the seated head's effect.

Use –5 to +5 N for this diagnostic. The 213.616 N archived reaction was obtained on the negative-X, positive-Y blade of one 500 g U1 simulation state. It supplies the earlier approximately 214 N envelope, with a +212.842 N axial component in that case. A higher-load trial needs a verified fixture, appropriate end support and a confirmed compression load path. A long curved blade under compression can deform substantially before the joint is assessed. Use a separately qualified single-end specimen and a fixture that controls the axial load line and out-of-plane movement. The present model covers straight axial fit and free-span elasticity. Hardware strength, torque retention, fatigue and loaded motion of the full robot remain open. ## Trace signs and reproduction

Signed force is positive in tension. Near and far leaf travel are positive towards the interior of the corresponding coupon. Proximal shoe and fastener travel are positive outward. Datum spacing is the absolute distance between the two PA12 faces. Use `seated_screw`, `fixed_pin` or `seated_screw_repeat` for the setup. Use `tension_loading`, `compression_loading` or `return` for each ramp and record the observed contact in the final column. The hardware CSVs have fields for observations. The virtual trace contains predictions for comparison.

Run from the repository or unpacked bench bundle with Python 3.12. Install the Python packages and versions recorded in the result summary. Use `python cad/lanes_u4m2/u_leaf_mechanical_joint/bench_reversal/run.py` from the top-level directory. The run imports the archived curved strip solver and compares the trial at 1.00 and 0.50 mm axial section spacing. Allow up to three minutes on the tested cloud configuration. Source hashes in the result identify the inputs. [Archived strip model](../../../../simulations/u4m2/extreme_tests/round1_20261119/u_springs.py)
