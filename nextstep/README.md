# Next steps for the 500 g U4+M2 front

The extreme-test programme asks four separate questions. What motion can the installed robot complete and reverse? Where does a permanent change first occur? What causes loss of load capacity? How many complete missions can the assembly sustain? The limiting event can differ among those four questions.

## Configuration and test basis

Keep the head and adapter at **500 g total**. Use the four 96 mm U modules with four installed NiTi blades in each, two M modules with independent Ø1.8 mm NiTi backbones, the eight installed guide stations and the twelve Ø2 mm ropes in the 21/24/28 mm lanes. Keep the current rope anchors, sleeve bonds and shared C-rope passage. Use the retained M5 guide seats and the actual CAD parts. Record the revision, as-built dimensions, head centre of mass, harness route, material batch and fixture orientation with each result.

One reference mission covers loaded startup and straightening, insertion, right and left yaw with returns, upward and downward pitch with returns, and complete withdrawal. The [archived twelve-rope data](../simulations/u4m2/lanes_20261113/cycle_closeout_20261119/results/yaw_pitch/ROPE_TENSIONS.md) contain 3,405 equilibrium states across **one** mission. Operating criteria include a 2.5 N distal-rope floor, a 1° pointing allowance and at least 5 mm body/tank clearance after the 1.5 mm allowance. Use measured drive and component ratings for upper loads. The controller's 0.2° settling threshold and 250 N numerical ceiling describe the simulation.

## Test sequence

| Stage | Test | Decision and deliverables |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Identify critical operating conditions | Rank the entire saved path separately for all twelve rope forces, guide force and moment, tank and local CAD gaps, and module curvature. Inspect M6 at step 1,710, the two M5 seats at 1,843 and 2,059, and the +69° pitch crossings at 2,348 and 2,432. Evaluate a U module in both principal bending directions. Apply simultaneous forces, moments and end pose. | Locate the first modelled loss of margin under each stated assumption. Deliver the step, pose, all ropes, location, assumptions and a testable load or offset. The [first-round report](../simulations/u4m2/lanes_20261113/extreme_round1_20261119/README.md) records this stage. |
| 2. Map the maximum usable motion | First sweep yaw through 0°, ±45°, ±90°, ±135°, ±160° and ±175°. Sweep elevation from −90° through −60°, −30°, 0°, +20°, +45°, +69° to +90°. Then vary yaw and elevation together, insertion depth, fixture tilt and bounded head eccentricity and harness force, with total head mass fixed. Compare free space and the installed tank. Test reaching, holding, reversing and withdrawing at each candidate boundary. | Map the last pass and first fail along each trajectory and name the limiting component. Initially target brackets of 1° or 1 mm, refining locally. At a vertical pole compare 3-D axes. Use curvature, twist and actuator stroke for additional mechanical travel. Track lengths, both rope-end tensions, guide loads and clearance at each substep. Recheck failed numerical continuations before assigning a physical limit. |
| 3. Determine first damage and ultimate capacity | Apply measured or validated service wrenches in local models and matching sacrificial fixtures. Cover U blades, pockets, bonds and Octo-Flex roots, M5 pin/relieved web/fork, fixed M6 guide/cap/wire, bellmouth bridges, rope crimps, M backbone sleeves, joints and head/base retention. Start at 0.50, 0.75, 1.00 and 1.25 times the selected local service load, then use smaller displacement-controlled increments around damage. Unload and inspect permanent set, slip and crack growth. | Obtain independent first-set, first-crack, peak-load and separation/pullout points, load paths, force–displacement curves, locations and uncertainty. Calibrate nonlinear PA12, NiTi and bond/contact laws with matched samples and verify on a held-out specimen. Local overloads are applied in a qualified, guarded fixture with the mission payload held at 500 g. |
| 4. Test tolerances, timed motion and faults | Measure friction in both directions, backlash, rope stiffness, printed fit, support stiffness and bond variation. Sweep measured low/high bounds or justified distributions. Use μ = 0, 0.05, 0.15 and 0.30 as sensitivity inputs pending calibration. Measure drive torque, response delay, speed and damping. Test nominal, slower and faster commands in simulation. Inject one slack/broken rope, jammed guide, locked winch, biased sensor, released spring end or entry obstruction at a time. | Identify the parameter combinations that first lose a usable margin. Record overshoot, residual oscillation, loads in the other ropes, detection and stopping distances, and ability to retreat. Progress from coupons to single modules to a supported dummy head in a guarded mock tank. |
| 5. Determine fatigue, wear and creep life | Build the service spectrum in complete missions, with measured dwell times, minor corrections, reversals and hours under tension. Test the NiTi blade and rod strain ranges at their installed mean strain, PA12 in its actual build direction and conditioning, bonded interfaces under shear/peel, and rope/crimp abrasion over a real bellmouth. A pilot can start with at least three independent specimens per condition at 0.8×, 1.0× and 1.2× local service amplitude. Inspect at 1, 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000 missions or equivalent blocks. | Report cycles to repeatable functional failure, observed first crack and rupture separately, including surviving specimens and uncertainty. Use rainflow counting and a damage sum only for calibrated curves and verify sequence effects with mission blocks. Treat wear and creep using travel and dwell data. Set required life from missions per shift, shifts per year and years of service. |
| 6. Rate the operating range | For each calibrated mode use lower-bound resistance divided by upper-bound demand in matching units. For nonlinear/buckling cases increase a specified physical disturbance on the preloaded assembly, solving equilibrium at every increment. Report rope tension margins in N, clearance in mm, and lower-bound mission life divided by required life. | Proposed **normal-service review targets** are ≥1.5 to first irreversible damage and ≥2.0 to ultimate failure. Agree duty, confidence, fault response and targets before rating the design. Supply a limit map, factor table, inspection/replacement intervals and open evidence. |

## Evidence needed before a rating

Use matched PA12 prints and conditioning, actual NiTi strips and rods, production rope/crimp pairs, measured adhesive preparation/cure and identified metal grades. Synchronize winch force and stroke, distal force on instrumented routes, 3-D head pose, guide motion and fixture loads. Use optical strain or gauges at predicted hotspots. At a critical load compare two spatial discretizations, load-step sizes and an independent sample. A proposed numerical target is less than 5% change in the finite-gauge strain and damage multiplier with the same failure location. A mesh peak with no material calibration cannot establish a breaking load.

Record last pass and first fail, measured versus assumed inputs, test direction, calibration, build revision, source hashes and uncertainty. Count functional failure separately from a crack or specimen rupture. Preserve incomplete runs and unknown margins. The [P3 witness record](../simulations/u4m2/lanes_20261113/p3_pin_fixture_20261119/witness_measurements.csv) and the [500 g validation plan](../docs/500g_validation_plan.md) provide the starting bench procedures.
