**20 November, free-space limits and repeated service.** With a 500 g head and nominal guide alignment, the 8-cell model reversed after ±296° heading. Local fixed-command checks with 16 cells put the first failed heading at ±298°. Both models first failed at +216° and −150° on the zero-yaw pitch paths. These limits depend on the controller and exit geometry. The M5 pin crossed a 205 MPa reference at 109.69–110.63° of leftward steering. Its installed grade, the failure strain of the web and the endurance of the finished ropes remain unmeasured. The active reset passed two simulated service blocks at matched friction and play. Physical fracture angle, service hours and a replacement interval remain open. [Engineering results](../reports/projects/extreme_tests/Free_Space_and_Service_Life_2026_11_20.md) · [Bench measurements](../nextstep/README.md#free-space-and-repeated-duty-follow-up)

**19 November, second extreme-test round at 500 g.** The 0.25 mm M6 candidate clears the nominal body triangles throughout the mission. The support model moves its rope bores by 2.07–2.18 mm in three critical poses. Print and measure the assembled guide position under load before relying on the ideal rope path. With the archived commands, simulated friction and drum play reduce the weakest distal rope to 1.44 N against a 2.5 N operating floor. The M5 pin also reaches 372.6 MPa of elastic bending demand at the more severe service load. Installed pin grade, print strength, bond properties and matched fatigue curves still determine the physical margins and lifetime. **19 November follow-up.** Coupled M5 shell and bearing calculations now impose a common guide motion at each ±12 mm station. A 0.15 mm inner hub chamfer improves the limiting bent pair. The physical coupon, cap engagement, installation torque, print scatter and post-cycle shift remain unmeasured. Four C ropes can slide laterally in the shared M5/M6 bore model. Bench friction, contact at other guide mouths, integration of all as-built stations and the complete hardware motion cycle remain open. **18 November follow-up (test 2, stage 2).** Shortening the two M5 fork tips cleared the returning web in the six tested nominal CAD states. The smallest remaining rib-to-hub gap is 0.044 mm. A fine 3-D contact patch predicts up to 6.06% incremental tensile strain and 242 MPa peak nodal pressure. The local peaks remain mesh-sensitive. The four pivot corrections require a shared-guide equilibrium. A 1.15 mm high side-drive envelope clears the 16 hub lap screws in CAD. **18 November Evening Report (test 2, stage 1).** This stage sizes the retained seat from the nonlinear states. The V3 relieved socket shape cannot host a rotating seat, because the 34°-inclined web swings into its walls. A pivot-centred bow-tie cavity with a Ø0.6 mm spherical-release pin clears all six load cases in simulation. The pin carries a reversing along-web force of up to 12.3 N in the holds and 24.7 N in the 96° bend, with a mesh-converged hole-edge strain of 2.4–2.7%. The analysis has no contact model and no seat CAD. Printed PA12 strength at the hole, pin clearance and binding, assembly and hardware all remain unverified. **17 November Evening Report:** The [rib-level joint model](../reports/daily/Evening_Report_2026_11_17.md) supersedes the homogenised slip estimate in the 16 November entry below for the ±12 mm joint. A rigid bonded land would carry about 8 N axial, 7.5 N radial and tangential, and up to 100 N·mm per land at the worst hold, and would stiffen M5 by 10–12%. The analysis is linear (module rotation about 1.68 rad), with ideal joints, rigid rings and guides, and no rope loads applied. Nonlinear behaviour, PA12 strain limits (7.8% linear peak strain) and any physical joint design remain unverified.

**16 November Evening Report:** A [full-coverage guide/web screen](../reports/daily/Evening_Report_2026_11_16.md) finds V3 body contact in 59/217 saved holds. It also shows that a rigid bonded land must absorb up to 0.087 mm axial slip in the homogenised beam map (58% shear at 0.15 mm gap, 35% at 0.25 mm). Strut-level deformation, adhesive capability and mount compliance are unmodelled; the 0.25 mm variant removes kinematic contact only.

**16 November Morning Report:** The [latest constrained bellmouth model](../reports/daily/Morning_Report_2026_11_16.md) solves all 217 selected fixed-winch holds and passes 5208 rigid-end envelope checks within 0.005 mm mesh tolerance. The reconstructed old commands shift the head by up to 3.479 mm and 1.147° yaw. All six targeted outer-saddle checks still interfere at 0.25 mm CAD resolution. **15 November Morning Report:** The [Morning Engineering Report](../reports/daily/Morning_Report_2026_11_15.md) corrects the long-triangle error in the deformed-CAD contact screen and documents a [V3 socket-relief candidate](../cad/lanes_u4m2/guides_v3/README.md). The 5% strain threshold and 250 N tendon bound are numerical diagnostics, not validated hardware ratings. Positive fixed-winch tangent stiffness does not establish dynamic closed-loop stability. The 1.5 mm mesh/sampling allowance is not a certified global clearance bound.

## Rope routing inside the modules (13 November 2026)

- Ropes are modelled as straight chords between module end disks. That is accurate for the U modules (≤ 7.3° bend, chords within 0.2 mm of the lane). In M5, chords leave the lane by 4 mm at the 0° hold, and by 7–33 mm when steering beyond 15° yaw (M5 bends up to 103°). They would then touch the M spring strips (6.7–7.8 mm corridor clearance in `cad/lanes_u4m2`). Results beyond about 15° yaw assume rope paths that the current M body cannot provide without intermediate guides.
- The lane-routed CAD (`cad/lanes_u4m2`) is checked geometrically only, in the straight pose: corridor clearance, 0.5 mm minimum wall between lane holes, free space for the anchor blocks, sleeves and joint screws. Part strength, rope friction and the stiffness of the re-clocked bodies are untested.
